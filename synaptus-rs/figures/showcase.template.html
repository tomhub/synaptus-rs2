Where Rust goes further

A sharper MULOK

MULOK is the fast multi-layer algorithm. While porting it we compared the original code line by line with the PhD thesis it comes from, and found three places where they differ. The Rust crate keeps a faithful copy of the original, and adds a version that follows the thesis with better interpolation. Below, each is compared with a very fine reference image of the same scan. Bright means wrong.

smaller overall envelope error on the pins-in-plexiglass-and-aluminium scan, measured against PSM with an 8× finer frequency grid. Later, the second Rust version found that this reference is itself slightly off. Against the exact answer the gap is 3.0% versus 0.06%, about 50×. Still a win; just a smaller trophy.

raccoon

The raccoon went through the frequency bins, which is what raccoons do with bins, and came back with three things the original threw away or mislabelled:

A squared correction

The amplitude factor for changing coordinates is applied squared in the code, where the thesis has it once.

dossier C4

A trimmed depth grid

The code's depth-frequency grid starts at the lowest transmitted frequency instead of zero, so some oblique echoes are thrown away.

dossier C5

A frequency shifted by ¾ of a step

After upsampling, the frequency axis is labelled 0.75 steps away from where the samples really are.

dossier C6

The cylindrical PSM got the same treatment. The original pretends a 64° scan is a full circle, lets evanescent waves through with zero phase and keeps the DC bin. The Rust default fixes all three; the faithful copy stays available for comparisons like the one above. The pipe image's brightness changes by about 3%, and almost nothing moves. Nobody said fixing bugs has to be dramatic.

Matching the original shows the port is faithful; it does not prove the original is right. That is why the point-reflector tests exist, and why the MULOK differences above were measured rather than copied.
